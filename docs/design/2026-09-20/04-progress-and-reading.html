04 — Wakati anafanya kazi

The waiting state, made of real steps instead of three pulsing dots. Today the app cycles three fixed sentences on a 1.8 s timer while the sweep animation loops forever and nothing is tied to the request — so a dead request and a healthy one look identical for as long as the user is willing to wait. Here the rail reports what actually happened, the reading of a file reports its real stage, and a slow connection is stated rather than hidden behind a spinner.

↻ Jaribu kuisoma tena Sawa

Sababu hii inatoka kwa seva — sitabuni nyingine.

1

Every step is a fact, not a timer

Today the “ramp” is setInterval(1800) cycling three hard-coded sentences (scan-processing-screen.tsx:27-28, 57-60), looping back to step 1 forever. It cannot be right, and it cannot be wrong. Each line here is emitted when the work behind it finishes — the OCR returns N characters, the advert yields its requirements, the comparison starts.

2

The unfinished steps are still named

A step that has not run yet is not a blank future. “Nitakupa kilichokosekana” tells the user what is coming, so a 20-second wait is a plan rather than a silence.

3

Acha — and the words survive it

The rail carries a stop control from the first second, and the composer becomes the stop button. Interrupting takes the partial work (the file has already been read; that is not thrown away) and never discards what the user typed — today editable={!loading} locks the field (chat-composer.tsx:142) and the three dots are un-interruptible (withRepeat(…,-1), sumaiya-chat-screen.tsx:39-50).

4

Elapsed time, quiet and small

inaendelea · 6s. It is the one number that makes a wait feel bounded, and it is a plain text node — no count-up animation (motion standard §4: numbers the user is comparing are not animated).

5

A slow connection is named, not hidden

The app knows: the client retries twice with backoff (attachment-extract.ts:42, 309) and maps timeouts to their own reason (:51-53). That knowledge reaches the screen as one line. Nothing is queued silently and no spinner is allowed to run with no end.

6

“Nilichosoma” — the sheet the trust case demands

The preview sheet already exists (attachment-preview-sheet.tsx) and already shows the extracted text and the real failure reason in Swahili. What it does not do is show the stages: today it prints a static refresh-cw icon beside “Ninasoma faili…” (:127-128) — an icon that never moves, over a state that can last a minute. Here the two stages are the actual upload and the actual extraction, and the failure box below them is the app's own cause-specific copy.

7

The bar counts finished steps — it cannot outrun the work

Two of four done is scaleX(.5), not a bar racing to the end while the third step is still running. The unfinished part of the current step lives in that step's own glyph (a bounded 1.05s beat), so motion is attached to the thing that is actually moving. transform only, never width. The old sweep is withRepeat(…, -1, true) with no cleanup (scan-processing-screen.tsx:43-45) — an unbounded per-frame callback on a device with a third of a Pixel's throughput. Reduced motion: no beat, the text carries everything.

8

Screen B and screen C are two different screens, not one panel with a footnote

A first draft of this mockup showed the extracted text and a “could not extract” warning together — two contradictory states in one sheet, reviewed and rejected. A succeeded read shows the text and a quiet Niliisoma yote. A failed read is screen C: error polarity on the sheet's top edge, the failure with its own icon and its own line, no extracted text, and the reason attributed to the server rather than invented (“Sababu hii inatoka kwa seva — sitabuni nyingine”) — exactly what attachment-extract.ts:46-87 already provides per failure code.

What this replaces, and why it matters more than it looks

A fake ramp is worse than no ramp. A user who watches three sentences cycle twice learns that the animation is not about their request; the third time they stop believing the screen and leave. Everything here is either a fact from the request, or a promise the app can keep.

Replaced: the 1.8 s setInterval sentence carousel; the unbounded 1.6 s sweep; the static refresh-cw; the un-interruptible three-dot bubble.
