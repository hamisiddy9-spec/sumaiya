# Research pass B — the STRUCTURE of a great "what are you?" answer

You are a positioning analyst. Use WebSearch and WebFetch to read real landing pages. **Cite the URL you read
for every claim**, and quote the actual sentence you are analysing.

Do NOT read or modify any files in this repository. Write nothing but your answer text.

## Task

Go to the live homepages of these products and extract, verbatim, the *first thing each one says about
itself* — the H1/hero headline and the subhead immediately under it:

- Stripe (stripe.com)
- Linear (linear.app)
- Notion (notion.so)
- Duolingo (duolingo.com)
- Canva (canva.com)
- Revolut (revolut.com)
- Figma (figma.com)
- Wise (wise.com)
- Shopify (shopify.com)
- Grammarly (grammarly.com)

Then, if you can find them, do the same for any African-market or emerging-market consumer product whose
homepage is written for a low-bandwidth, mobile-first audience — for example M-Pesa (vodacom.co.tz or
safaricom.co.ke), Airtel Money, Tala, Branch, Fuzu, Moniepoint, OPay, PiggyVest, or a comparable local
leader. Say plainly if you could not read them (paywall, bot-blocking, JS-only rendering) rather than
guessing from memory.

## Then do the analytical work — this is the point of the pass

1. **Classify each headline by what it claims.** Is it an outcome ("X, delivered"), a category ("the new
   operating system for Y"), a verb/action, a metaphor, a negation ("banking is not a place"), or a name
   plus a category? Tabulate it.

2. **Extract the STRUCTURE, not the tone.** For each, decompose the hero into its slots and label them. Test
   this proposed skeleton against every example and say where it fails:
   SHAPE + WHO-FOR + OUTCOME, with the mechanism either absent or subordinate.
   Which slot does each headline actually fill? Which slots are omitted, and what does omitting that slot buy?

3. **Length and register.** Word counts. Second person ("you/your") vs first person ("we"). Present tense.
   Concrete nouns vs abstractions. Quantify with a table — people overestimate how long these are.

4. **What the subhead is FOR.** Look at what the subhead adds that the headline does not. Is it the mechanism,
   the proof, the audience, or the relief of an objection? Give the pattern and the exceptions.

5. **The negative space rule.** Find the examples whose headline deliberately refuses a category the reader
   would assume. What is the cost of that refusal?

6. **What these pages do NOT say.** Note what is absent: feature lists above the fold, adjectives like
   "powerful"/"seamless"/"revolutionary", claims of being an "AI platform", team bios, awards. Give evidence
   for each absence you assert.

7. **Then write the skeleton explicitly** — a numbered, reusable recipe for answering "what is this?" in one
   line for a product whose users are on cheap Android phones with prepaid data, reading Swahili, and whose
   competition is a human CV writer and a WhatsApp group. Address specifically: what changes when the reader
   is not a Silicon Valley knowledge worker, and what does NOT change.

## Output

One markdown document, quotes in quotation marks with their URL, tables where a table is clearer, and a final
section "Unverified / could not read" listing every page you failed to read and why.
