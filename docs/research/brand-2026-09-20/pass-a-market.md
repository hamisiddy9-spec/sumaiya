# Research pass A — the African career/jobtech market in 2026

You are a market researcher. Use WebSearch and WebFetch. **Cite a URL for every factual claim.** Where you
cannot find a source, write "NO SOURCE FOUND" rather than estimating a number. Never invent statistics.

Do NOT read or modify any files in this repository. This is a pure research task; write nothing except your
answer text.

## The product you are researching for (read once, then forget the name)

An offline-first, Swahili-first CV-and-job-application product for Tanzania, expanding across East Africa.
Users are on cheap Android phones, prepaid data, unreliable connections. The core journey: a person builds a
CV, tailors it to a real job advert, applies, and gets hired. A guest can finish and download a CV (a real
PDF) before being asked for an account. No payments are live yet. The mobile app is offline-capable; the web
is the reachable surface. WhatsApp is a major communication channel in this market.

## Answer these questions, in this order

### 1. Who actually serves the African career/jobtech market today (2025–2026)?
For each of these, give: who owns it now, whether it is alive, which countries it really operates in, how it
makes money, and any dated evidence of change (acquisition, withdrawal, pivot):
Jobberman (Nigeria/Ghana), BrighterMonday (Kenya/Uganda — note what happened to the Tanzania domain),
Fuzu (Finland-founded), MyJobMag, Ajira Digital (Kenya — confirm it is Kenyan, not Tanzanian, and say what it
actually is), Ajira Portal / Public Service Recruitment Secretariat (Tanzania government), Zoom Tanzania,
Jobwebtanzania, Careergo, SwiftKazi, TATC / TATC Africa, and any pan-African entrant that appeared in 2025 or
2026 that I have not named.

### 2. LinkedIn's real penetration in Tanzania, Kenya, Nigeria and Ghana
Find actual usage figures (users, share of jobseekers, share of recruitment). If the closest thing available
is an adult-internet-user share from DataReportal, We Are Social/Meltwater or similar, give it and label it as
that. If nothing credible exists for Tanzania, say so explicitly. Do not round up.

### 3. WhatsApp-based job distribution
How are jobs actually distributed on WhatsApp in Kenya, Nigeria, Ghana and Tanzania — named public groups,
channels, aggregator bots, employer groups? Find dated examples with URLs if they exist. Then: is there any
published evidence about job-search behaviour (not political engagement, not general messaging preference) on
WhatsApp in East Africa? If not, say "no study found" plainly.

### 4. What jobseekers in Tanzania, Kenya, Nigeria and Ghana actually complain about
Find real, quotable evidence: Reddit threads, Twitter/X threads, news articles, app-store review text,
NGO/ILO/gov reports, academic papers. For each complaint, give the source URL and the country. Distinguish:
(a) complaints about job boards, (b) complaints about CV services / CV writers, (c) complaints about employers
never replying, (d) complaints about scams and fraudulent job adverts. If a claim is only an opinion piece,
label it as such.

### 5. Data cost, device reality and offline use
Dated figures for mobile-data affordability and smartphone/screen reality in Tanzania and its neighbours
(ITU, GSMA, TCRA, Bank of Tanzania, World Bank). Specifically: what fraction of monthly income does a 1–2 GB
data bundle cost, and how do people work around it (offline apps, WhatsApp bundles, night bundles, cafés)?
This decides whether an "offline-first" claim is meaningful or marketing.

### 6. Where does an offline-first, Swahili-first CV-and-application product genuinely fit?
Given all of the above: name the specific gap. Be honest about (a) gaps that are already occupied — e.g. free
Swahili CV builders that already exist — and (b) gaps that look open but are not really winnable (e.g. the
government recruitment portal). Rank the three strongest realistic openings, and for each say what would have
to be true for it to work.

### 7. What nobody in this market does
List capabilities no player in the market offers today (candidate outcome tracking, response-rate data,
offline CV-building, Swahili-first product depth, whatever the evidence supports). Cite or say NO SOURCE.
