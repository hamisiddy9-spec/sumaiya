# Pass 2 — Neema, 24, Mwanza: what I see on that screen

I'm answering as myself, not as some "target user." I finished VETA in 2024, I've never
had a contract job, and I have about 55 MB of data a day that I guard like it's money —
because it is.

## 1. My friend shows me the screen

First thing I notice isn't even the words — it's the `/ ... /` around the sentence. That
looks broken. Like someone forgot to finish the page, or like a template that didn't load
properly. When I see something that looks half-built, my first thought is "hii app bado
haijakamilika" (this app isn't finished yet), not "hii ni bidhaa nzuri." If a friend
showed me this on WhatsApp I'd assume she screenshotted a page that glitched.

Once I read past that: "CV inayokufikisha kwa mwajiri" — okay, a CV that reaches the
employer. Fine, that's the job. Then the smaller line explains it properly: paste the job
ad, it writes you a CV that matches it, in Swahili, on your phone, free, no account. That
part I understand. That's a real thing I'd want.

What do I think it costs? Nothing, going by the words — but here's the thing, every scam
group I've seen on those job WhatsApp groups also says "bure" in the first line. "Bure"
is the bait word. So my honest answer is: the words say free, but I don't believe words,
I believe what happens when I press the button. If it asks me for my name and phone
number before it shows me anything, that's already one step closer to the fee-request
pattern I've seen burn two people I know personally.

**My first fear**, in order:
1. It's going to ask for money after I've already typed my whole CV in — like it holds
   the download hostage. That's the oldest trick.
2. It wants my phone number to add me to a group or resell my number to those "job
   placement" callers.
3. It's another site "built for Nairobi" — meaning it'll be slow, it'll ask for a card,
   or the Swahili will read like Google Translate and I'll close it in ten seconds.

**Would I give it my data bundle?** For one try, yes — a small one, maybe 5–10 MB to see
what happens. Not because I trust the copy, but because "kwenye simu yako" and "bila
akaunti" are testable claims — I can find out in one attempt whether they're true. I
would not sit and build a whole CV, attach a photo, and answer twenty questions before I
know if it charges me at the end. I test small before I commit data.

## 2. Jobs feed, CV check, tracking, interview, lessons — does that change anything?

Blunt answer: right now it reads as more stuff, not more reason to trust it.

Here's why. The screen already promised me one very specific thing — a CV that matches
this job ad, right now. If in the same breath it's also a jobs feed, a CV checker, an
application tracker, a career interview, and a school, my first reaction isn't "wow,
everything I need" — it's "which one of these is it actually good at?" I've used enough
half-finished apps to know that five mediocre things usually means no one thing works
well. And on a cheap phone, more features usually means more loading, more permissions,
more taps before I get to the one thing I opened it for.

That said — two of those aren't "more stuff" to me, they're the same job. **A jobs feed**
and **application tracking** are literally the next two steps after the CV: find the ad,
apply, remember who I applied to and who ghosted me. If those are wired together — I see
a job, I press one thing, it makes me the matching CV, it remembers I applied — that's
not feature creep, that's just... doing the job hunt. Don't sell it to me as five
products. Sell it to me as one path: *ona kazi → tengeneza CV inayolingana → tuma →
kumbuka umetuma wapi*.

**"Career interview"** is the one that sounds heaviest and vaguest to me. If it means "we
ask you fifteen personal questions before you get any value," that's a toll gate, and
I'll bounce. If it means "answer three quick things once, and every CV after that gets
faster," fine — but the copy needs to say that up front, or I'll assume it's homework.

**Learning lessons** — I'm not against it, but if I open an app to write a CV tonight
because there's a job ad closing tomorrow, and it tries to enroll me in a course first,
that's the wrong moment. Put it where I'd go looking for it, not in my way.

## 3. What gets me to open it a second time / tell someone

Second open, for me, comes down to one test: **did the first thing actually finish and
leave my hands with something real** — a PDF I can attach in WhatsApp or email, that
opens properly on my phone, that reads like it was written by someone who knows this job,
not a robot. If I got that, for free, without giving a card or a password, I'll open it
again without thinking twice.

I'll tell someone else if one of two things happens: the CV actually got me a reply or an
interview call (that's the only proof that matters in my groups — "ilinifanyia kazi"), or
if it flatly did NOT try to squeeze money or a phone number out of me at the point where a
scam usually does. In my circles, "haikuniomba pesa wala namba" is itself a recommendation
— trust is the product as much as the CV is.

## 4. The one sentence I wish it said

Swahili, the way I'd actually say it to a friend:

> **"Bandika tangazo la kazi, upate CV inayolingana nalo — bure kabisa, bila akaunti, bila
> kadi."**

English:

> "Paste the job ad, get a CV that matches it — completely free, no account, no card."

Why this over the current one: "inayokufikisha kwa mwajiri" (reaches the employer) is a
promise about an outcome I can't verify in the moment — it sounds like the kind of thing
a poster says about itself. "Bila kadi" (no card) is the one line that would actually kill
my fear, because the card is the exact thing I've never had and the exact thing every real
scam eventually asks for. Say the thing I'm scared of, by name, and deny it, instead of a
soft "it reaches the employer."

## 5. Exactly where the wording loses me

- The `/ ... /` around the headline. Whatever it's meant to signal in a design file, on my
  screen it reads as broken formatting. That's the very first trust hit, before I've read
  a single word of Swahili.
- **"inayokufikisha kwa mwajiri"** — "reaches the employer" is vague. Reaching isn't
  getting hired. It's a promise I can't check, so it just sits there sounding nice.
- **"bila malipo, bila akaunti"** without **"bila kadi"** — free and no-account is exactly
  what the WhatsApp scam posts also say. Missing the one specific reassurance (no card,
  since I don't have one and never will) is the gap.
- **"Snap-to-Apply"** in the Swahili nav bar, sitting next to "Ajira," "Zana za PDF,"
  "Kagua CV." That's one English phrase dropped into a Swahili list, and it reads foreign
  — like the rest of the nav was translated and this one wasn't finished. Small thing, but
  it's exactly the kind of small thing that makes me think the app is built for someone
  else and I'm an afterthought.
- **"CV & Career AI"** style naming anywhere I'd see it (Play Store) leads with the
  mechanism, not what I get. I don't know what "AI" does for me and I don't need to. Tell
  me I get hired faster, don't tell me what's under the hood.
